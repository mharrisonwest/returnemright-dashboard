1. Historical Discards

Data shown in this tab is displayed in order to show trends in discards, releases, and dead discards in fisheries of interest. More details are available in this document.

Data sources: Data on annual discards (releases) and landings were provided by NOAA by year by sector and region (Table 1). These data were used as inputs by the most recent stock assessment for each species and region (Gulf of America [hereafter ‘Gulf’ or South Atlantic [hereafter ‘Atlantic’]). Additionally, Gulf estimates were categorized as open or closed season and by area (east, central, west) for red snapper only. Data were categorized by sectors and regions used for the stock assessments and are aggregated by region, sector, and season for the purpose of display in this tool.

The following estimates are displayed:

  1. Dead Upon Release – the number of fish discarded that are estimated to have died. This was calculated by multiplying the discards by the discard mortality estimate used in the stock assessment (see Appendix A).
  2. Released Alive – The number of fish released that were estimated to live. This was calculated by subtracting the fish ‘dead upon release’ from the discards.
  3. Fish Kept – Landings, as provided by NOAA.

2. Descender Device Use

Information shown in this tab provides an estimate of the number of fish that would have survived release each year if descenders had been used on the given percentage of releases. These numbers are calculated by combining empirical information on the condition of fish released with information from research studies comparing mortality rates of fish released with descending devices and fish released at the surface. These estimates were calculated using methods similar to those used for analyses presented in Vecchio et al. (2020 and 2022), which were incorporated into the most recent red snapper stock assessments for the Gulf and Atlantic.

These estimates are combined with observer data describing the frequency of fish released in different conditions to derive a maximum benefit to each fishery (e.g. mortality under 100% descender use for fish suffering from barotrauma). The survival increase associated with different levels of descender device usage was calculated by assuming a linear relationship between the potential survival benefit and the rate of descender device usage. Estimates are provided for specific years of data based on the historical releases reported (data from the ‘Historical Discards’ tab).
